# Changelog

All notable changes to the Leviton LDATA integration are documented here.

## 2.0.14 - Unreleased

**Take a backup before updating.** Going back to an earlier version re-creates every entity as a new one without history, so the only way back is a backup. (#123)

### Added
- **The load center can have its own page in the sidebar.** A new option, **Show the load center in the sidebar**, adds a sidebar item that opens a full page with the panel card, with no dashboard to set up. With more than one load center, each has its own tab. It is off by default and can be turned on at setup or in the integration's options; the item appears or disappears as soon as you save. With several LDATA entries, one item shows the load centers of every entry that has the option on. On a phone, a button in the page's header zooms the panel to 1.5× or 2×. (#118)
- **Sub-panels are set once, in the integration's options.** With two or more load centers, a new **Sub-panels** section lets you choose the breaker that feeds each sub-panel. That breaker then shows the sub-panel's live load on every panel card, and clicking it opens the sub-panel: in a popup on a dashboard, on its own tab on the sidebar page, which links back with "Fed from". A smart breaker can be a feeder too; it keeps its own readings and its detail sheet leads to the sub-panel. Give the size of a feeder that isn't a smart breaker, and the feeder's label gets a load bar and the sub-panel's header shows how much of the feeder it uses (a main panel does the same against the card's `main_breaker`). The card's `sub_panels` YAML keeps working and adds to this. (#118)
- **The panel card shows AFCI, GFCI and dual function breakers as they are, and says what tripped a breaker.** Those breakers are drawn with their `AF` and `GF` LEDs and `OFF/TEST` handle. After a trip the circuit's label names the cause (ground fault, arc fault, overload, short circuit) and the card lights the same LED the breaker does: `GF` for a ground fault, `AF` for an arc fault. A breaker that reports a fault in itself (a failed self-test, end of life or a GFCI error, on either pole of a 2-pole breaker) is drawn tripped with both flashing fast, and its label says it needs service, as Leviton's app does. The breaker's detail sheet also shows its protection type. (#118)
- **Translations for 20 languages.** The panel card, the sidebar page, the setup and options dialogs and the integration's actions are now available in Chinese (Simplified and Traditional), Czech, Danish, Dutch, Finnish, French, German, Hungarian, Italian, Norwegian Bokmål, Polish, Portuguese (Portugal and Brazil), Romanian, Russian, Spanish (Spain and Latin America), Swedish and Ukrainian, and follow the language in your Home Assistant profile. Entity names stay in English. (#118)
- **Firmware update entities for smart breakers.** Each breaker gets a firmware `update` entity, like the panel's, that turns on when Leviton has firmware waiting for it. It follows the My Leviton app: a breaker on an LDATA hub reports the update Leviton offers it, and a breaker on an LWHEM reports one once the panel has downloaded newer firmware for it, naming each part that changes. They are disabled by default, so a firmware rollout doesn't send the firmware notification blueprint's users one notification per breaker; enable the ones you want on the breaker's device page. Installing still happens in the My Leviton app. (#119)
- **Each load center's type and load capacity, in the integration's options.** Leviton doesn't report what a load center can carry. Under **Panel type and load capacity**, say whether each load center is a main panel or a sub-panel without a main breaker (fed from a main breaker by the meter, say), and enter its capacity in amps. The panel card then shows how much of it is in use, on the sidebar page and on dashboard cards that don't set `main_breaker` themselves, and draws a main panel's main breaker (Leviton's LM-T, with its rating printed on it). The main breaker is drawn next to positions 1 and 2, so at the bottom of a panel turned 180°, unless you choose the top or the bottom. (#126)

### Changed
- **Entity unique ids use your Leviton user id instead of your email.** Home Assistant's guidelines rule out an email as a unique id, and signing in again with the email typed in other capitals turned every entity into a new one. Existing entities move over at the first start, keeping their entity ids, names, areas and history; going back to an earlier version afterwards creates them anew. (#123)
- **The gap handling choices and the two actions (services) can be translated.** They were fixed English text before. (#118)
- **The Portuguese translation is now loaded.** Its file was named `pt-PT`, a code Home Assistant never asks for, so Portuguese users saw English. It is now `pt`, and complete. (#118)
- **The panel card now shows your Leviton load center as it looks on the wall.** It is assembled from ray-traced images modeled on Leviton's own product photos instead of being drawn, with every breaker in its real space. Breakers show what the hardware shows: a green handle window when on, white with the handle switched over when turned off by hand, red and centered when tripped, and a green handle with the "OFF" LED lit when switched off remotely (a Gen 1 breaker, drawn with its own face and the LEDs it blinks for Identify, has no "OFF" LED and is drawn tripped instead). Circuit labels beside the door show live Watts/Amps, and the line under each one fills with how close the circuit runs to its rating. The card is sized to your panel (20, 30, 42 spaces, ...) and to the card's width, with a phone layout that keeps circuit names readable. Your existing card configuration keeps working. (#118)
- **Switch a breaker by clicking its rocker; click its label or anywhere else on it for everything about that breaker.** The detail sheet has on/off and Identify (Blink LED), live power, current and load, energy, alarms, the raw breaker state and last change reason, Bluetooth signal and device details, and each row opens that entity's history. Clicking a breaker used to open its device page; the sheet links to it instead. (#118)
- **The panel card shows the panel's own readings above the door**: load, voltage and current per leg, line frequency, today's energy and your CT clamps, with pilot lights for the cloud connection, Wi-Fi signal and an available firmware update. While the panel is offline, or Leviton can't be reached, the cover says so, and each breaker is shown where it last was. An LDATA hub that is synchronizing no longer counts as offline. (#118)
- **New panel card options**: `confirm` (ask before switching, default on), `show_panel_info` (the readings above the door), `appearance` (follow the theme, or always bright or dimmed) and `main_breaker` (show a main breaker with its rating). The card can also be set up from the dashboard editor instead of YAML. (#118)
- **The panel status refresh runs every 110 seconds instead of 120.** Leviton's server closes idle connections at 120 seconds, so about 1 refresh in 20 hit a dropped connection and logged an error. (#111)
- **The reset energy services are admin-only.** **Reset energy sensor baseline** and **Reset panel energy** can no longer be called by non-admin users. (#121)
- **Home Assistant 2026.8 or newer is required.** The integration already needed it to add breakers and CTs; HACS now offers updates only to versions that can run them. (#121, see #120)
- **On panels with v1 firmware, breaker energy is polled every 60 seconds**, or at the update rate if that is longer, instead of at the update rate. At its 2-second minimum that sent three PATCH requests and a GET every 2 seconds per panel. (#121)
- **Decora entities no longer have `local_ip` and `signal_strength` attributes.** Each signal change wrote a new history row for every entity of the device. Use the device's Signal Strength sensor instead; `room` stays. (#122)

### Fixed
- **The diagnostics download no longer contains your email or your home's identifiers.** The email was in the entry's title and in every entity's id, and the residence id, the Leviton account id and Decora devices' local IP addresses were included too. Device ids, MAC addresses and serial numbers stay, since they are what the download is for. (#122)
- **Logs no longer include Leviton's responses or your account details.** Debug logs printed whole responses with your user, account and residence ids and residence names, a warning printed your email, and errors carried web addresses with your user id. "Log All Parsed Data" now also hides device ids and local IP addresses. (#122)
- **A breaker whose power is on no longer shows as off after a remote error.** Leviton reports a remote error, a switch-off that failed, an over-voltage report and its all-clear, and a switch-on in progress as remote states of their own. The breaker's switch and status showed all of them as off; they now show on, as the My Leviton app does. A breaker switched off for over-voltage, a switch-on that failed and a switch-off in progress still show off. (#119)
- **Metering Accessories no longer get a switch that does nothing, and their breaker is no longer shown as unmonitored.** A Leviton Metering Accessory (model LSBMA) meters a standard breaker, so it now gets no breaker control switch; its Blink LED switch still identifies it. The standard breaker it meters was also listed for the panel card as unmonitored, on the same spaces as its accessories; it no longer is. Untested on real hardware: the record layout comes from the My Leviton app's own code. (#119)
- **Lifetime energy no longer jumps by thousands of kWh on some hubs.** On a DAU or LDATA hub whose energy counters don't behave like true lifetime totals, a breaker's Consumption/Import sensor could leap far beyond what the breaker could physically draw after a restart, wrecking Energy dashboard totals. A reading the breaker couldn't have drawn since the last one is now ignored, and a panel whose counters keep decreasing, jumping, or reading far below the total already recorded switches to estimating energy from power over time, with a warning in the log. Readings repeated while a hub is offline are ignored, so after a long outage the first real reading is judged against the whole outage, not a minute of it. Use the **Reset energy sensor baseline** service to accept a value that was held back. (#115)
- **Quiet houses no longer cause constant WebSocket reconnects.** When nothing was drawing power for a few minutes, the integration mistook the silence for a dead connection and reconnected every 7 to 9 minutes, losing about 11 seconds of updates each time. It now treats Leviton's 30-second keepalive as proof the connection is alive. (#111, see #83)
- **Failed reconnects no longer hammer Leviton's servers.** If Leviton closed a new connection immediately, the integration retried about once a second until Leviton rate-limited it (HTTP 429). Retries now back off from 10 seconds up to 5 minutes. (#111)
- **Panel status refresh errors are retried quietly.** A dropped connection is retried once, and a warning is logged only after three failed refreshes in a row. (#111)
- **An expired Leviton sign-in is renewed by itself.** When Leviton stopped accepting the stored sign-in, live data froze until Home Assistant restarted, while every entity still looked available. The integration now signs in again with the stored credentials, and if Leviton refuses them it asks you to reauthenticate, with the entities unavailable until you do. (#124)
- **Signing in again with a different Leviton account is refused.** It moved the integration to that account, so every device and entity became a new one and the old ones were orphaned. Sign in with the account it was set up with, or add the other account as a new integration. (#125)
- **Saving integration options no longer wipes your solar settings or causes repeated reloads.** Saving options erased the solar and suppressed import-sensor lists, so solar import sensors could disappear and hidden ones could return. Each reload also added another listener, so after several reloads one options change reloaded the integration several times. It now reloads once, and only when an option actually changed. Unloading the integration now also stops its background tasks cleanly. (#110)
- **Daily energy now resets at midnight after a very low day.** A daily sensor that finished the day at 0.05 kWh or less didn't reset, so Home Assistant under-counted the next day. (#112)
- **The reset-panel-energy service now finds breaker sensors.** Choosing a breaker reset nothing, and choosing a panel or CT skipped every breaker sensor. (#112)
- **Leftover breaker devices from versions before 2.0.9 are cleaned up.** They held only dead entities and caused a "Could not resolve a panel or Decora room" warning per breaker at every startup. You can now also delete a device that is no longer in use from its device page. (#113, see #86)
- **The panel card updates after you upgrade the integration.** Browsers kept running a cached copy of the card from the first version you installed until you forced a refresh. The card's dashboard resource is now pointed at the current card at every startup. (#118)
- **Registering the panel card can no longer remove your other dashboard resources.** At startup the integration could read the dashboard resource list before Home Assistant had loaded it, which added a duplicate card entry on each restart or, if nothing else loaded the list in time, saved over your other custom card resources. It now loads the list first and removes duplicate card entries left by earlier versions. (#118)
- **LDATA panels now receive live updates over the WebSocket.** Panels were subscribed in a way that delivered nothing for LDATA hubs, so panel-level readings only refreshed on the periodic poll. They now update live, and Cloud Connected, frequency and Wi-Fi signal follow the same rules as the periodic refresh. (#114)
- **A breaker trip is no longer undone by a background poll** on panels with v1 firmware. A poll that took up to a minute wrote back the data it started with, undoing what the WebSocket had reported meanwhile. (#121)
- **Decora commands no longer knock panels off the cloud while the WebSocket is down.** The refresh each command asks for toggled every panel's bandwidth, which takes a hub offline for 10 to 20 seconds, and could still be running after Home Assistant gave up on it. (#121)
- **A command that fails shows an error.** Switching a breaker or its LED, or controlling a Decora device, logged a failure and carried on, so automations and dashboards took it for done. (#121)
- **A panel that is offline when Home Assistant starts is no longer missing until a reload.** It appears, with Cloud Connected off and its readings unknown, and fills in when the panel comes back. (#121)
- **A device without a name no longer stops the integration from loading.** A breaker, panel, CT or Decora device with no name, or a breaker with no pole count, gets a default. (#121)
- **Spurious import sensors are removed on panels with 2.2 firmware.** The cleanup looked them up by an id that firmware no longer uses. (#121)
- **Fewer database writes.** Many sensors wrote their state twice on every update. (#121)

## 2.0.13 - 2026-10-15

### Changed
- **Daily energy sensors no longer publish a `last_update_time` attribute.** It changed on every update, forcing a database write for each daily energy sensor even when the reading hadn't moved. Dropping it reduces database writes and Home Assistant event traffic. If you used that attribute in a template or automation, use the sensor's own `last_updated` instead. (#101)
- **Panel voltage shows "unknown" instead of 0 V when the panel doesn't report it.** The Voltage Leg 1 / Leg 2 sensors previously showed a misleading 0 V, which looked like a dead leg, whenever Leviton's data was missing a reading. A real 0 V reading is still shown as 0 V. (#107)
- **A wired LDATA hub no longer gets a Wi-Fi Signal sensor that can never show a value.** Hubs connected over Ethernet report no Wi-Fi signal, so the sensor sat on "unknown" forever. It is no longer created for them, and an existing one is removed on the next start. Wi-Fi hubs are unaffected, and the sensor now clears its reading instead of holding a stale one if a hub stops reporting a signal. (#105)

### Fixed
- **Firmware update entities are no longer filed as diagnostic.** Home Assistant categorized the panel and Decora firmware update entities as diagnostic by default, so they were listed under Diagnostic on the device page instead of with the device's main entities. They are still not counted in the "Updates available" sidebar badge or Settings card, which only count updates Home Assistant can install.
- **LDATA panel readings no longer freeze after setup.** Voltage, frequency, over/under-voltage alarms, Wi-Fi signal and firmware version on LDATA panels now refresh every couple of minutes instead of staying at their startup values until you reload the integration. Setup also no longer fails if a panel reports no firmware version. (#104)
- **Breaker details stay current.** The firmware, hardware, rating and model shown on each breaker, and the firmware version on its device page, now update after a breaker firmware change instead of waiting for a reload. (#106)
- **A panel reporting 0 V is no longer ignored on WHEMS panels.** A real 0 V leg reading now shows as 0 V instead of keeping the previous voltage. (#108)
- **Cloud Connected now turns off when your panel goes offline, and back on when it returns** — without reloading the integration. Previously it could stay "on" indefinitely, and Home Assistant could stop receiving updates altogether after one of Leviton's routine reconnects. Sensors still show their last known values while the panel is offline. (#103, fixes #102)
- **Your account password could be visible in the integration's name.** Setups created between April 2023 and October 2025 were titled with the password included, shown on the Devices & Services page and written to logs. These are now renamed automatically to `Leviton LDATA (your email)`, and a warning in the log tells you it happened. If yours was affected, consider changing your Leviton password — the old name may still be in past logs and backups. (#100)

## 2.0.12 - 2026-09-23

### Added
- **Firmware update entities for your panel and Decora Smart Wi-Fi devices**, using Home Assistant's native Update platform. When Leviton has a newer firmware version available, the entity turns on, so you can set up a notification automation instead of checking the My Leviton app. Because the integration cannot install firmware, Home Assistant does not count these entities in the "Updates available" sidebar badge or Settings card. Installing an update still happens there — this integration only reports availability.

### Changed
- **The breaker "Blink LED" switch is now opt-in**, behind a new **Allow Identify** option (off by default), separate from Allow Breaker Control. Previously it was always created, even on a fully read-only setup — if you use it to physically locate breakers, enable it under Settings > Devices & Services > Leviton LDATA > Configure after upgrading. (#91)
- **The panel's Firmware Update entity moved from a sensor to Home Assistant's Update platform** (`update.*` instead of `sensor.*`). If you had an automation or dashboard card on the old `sensor.<panel>_firmware_update` entity, it will stop updating after this upgrade — point it at the new `update.*` entity instead, then remove the orphaned old one from Settings > Devices & Services > Entities.

### Fixed
- **Keeping your panel's live updates flowing now matches Leviton's latest app (API 1.66.0).** Leviton quietly switched the internal request that keeps data flowing from PUT to PATCH — no visible symptoms yet, but this keeps the integration working if Leviton drops the old method.

## 2.0.11 - 2026-09-18

### Added
- **Breaker Over Voltage alarm** — a new sensor for each smart breaker, alongside the existing Over Current and Under Voltage alarms. (#94)
- **Breaker State diagnostic sensor** — shows Leviton's raw breaker state (e.g. manual shutoff vs. a software trip, AFCI/GFCI fault, overload, or short circuit) instead of a simplified on/off, plus details like trip reason and lock status. (#95)
- **Panel Firmware Update sensor** — shows whether a firmware update is available for your panel, and the currently installed version. Purely informational; installing an update still happens in the My Leviton app. (#96)

### Fixed
- **Daily energy no longer resets on restart.** Breaker and CT daily consumption/import totals were being wiped back to 0 every time Home Assistant restarted, even mid-day. Restored totals are now kept if they're genuinely from today. (#93)
- **Upgrading from a 1.x version could get stuck asking to re-authenticate.** Older installs are now migrated automatically using your already-saved login, instead of forcing a manual sign-in. (#92)
- **Decora Wi-Fi bridges no longer break device setup.** Accounts with a Leviton Wi-Fi bridge could fail to connect properly, and the bridge's "Identify" button didn't work. Bridges are now handled correctly and no longer risk being confused with another bridge on the same account. (#97)
- Panel, breaker, and Decora device firmware versions shown in Home Assistant now update live instead of only refreshing after a restart.
- **Panel firmware version now updates live.** The panel's installed firmware version, shown on the device page and used by the Firmware Update sensor, now refreshes as new data arrives instead of only after a restart. (follow-up to #96)
- Improved compatibility with upcoming Home Assistant releases by moving off two internal APIs that Home Assistant has marked for removal. No visible change today — this just keeps the integration working on future Home Assistant updates.
- **An expired login could force you to re-authenticate even though your saved password still worked.** This is now handled automatically — the integration retries your stored username and password before ever asking you to sign in again. (#98)
